01 — What LOAD|BY|TON.store is
LOAD|BY|TON.store is the commercial entry point to a modular logistics operating platform. A logistics company
registers once, selects its sectors, starts in a useful permanent free workspace, and purchases connected operational
bundles as its needs grow. The same company account becomes its branded operating environment; it does not
restart in a separate application when it upgrades.


The store sells software subscriptions, setup and support services. Its customers use the software to sell and deliver
freight, transport, clearance, storage or delivery services. These are separate commercial relationships and separate
accounting books. The store is not inherently a freight brokerage, cargo marketplace or carrier. Those activities would
need separate operating models if introduced later.


The principal buyer is the owner of a small or growing logistics business who wants affordable operational control
without commissioning bespoke software. The daily users are salespeople, coordinators, dispatchers, warehouse staff,
accountants, drivers and managers. Their customers, suppliers and agents interact through limited portals.


Public explanation: “Your logistics company, connected. Start free, choose the tools your business needs, and run
enquiries, jobs, documents, deliveries and collections from one branded workspace.”


The strategic objective is an accessible logistics company OS with sector-specific workflows, shared records and
evidence-backed owner visibility. “World-class” should mean reliable delivery of those outcomes, not a maximum
number of menus.

03 — Market focus and initial customer
Begin with UAE small freight forwarders that coordinate transport through suppliers and use email, WhatsApp and
spreadsheets. This is a proposed beachhead, to validate in interviews. It keeps the first paid workflow focused while
opening expansion into transport, customs and storage.


Interview 15–20 owners and operators. Ask them to demonstrate a real quotation, supplier purchase, job file, cost
update, invoice and collection. Measure duplicate entry, missing costs, time-to-invoice and reconciliation effort. Do not
rely only on “Would you use this?” answers. Obtain anonymized sample documents with permission.


An appropriate first design partner has one entity, a small operations team, repeat jobs and an owner willing to review
outcomes weekly. A poor first fit requires global customs coverage, airport-certified systems, complex bonded
warehouse automation or enterprise-wide migration before any value can be delivered.


Target expansion cohorts: transport SMEs; warehouse/3PL businesses; last-mile operators; fulfilment providers;
integrated logistics firms. Each requires its own completed workflow and acceptance pack. Market sizing must be built
from verified company counts and actual willingness-to-pay research; no market-share or revenue forecast is asserted
here.

05 — Commercial model
Revenue sources: recurring sector subscriptions; additional full users, branches and volume; scoped setup; historical
migration; training; connector configuration; dedicated hosting; premium support. Avoid relying on one-off custom
development to fund a low-price SaaS model.


Keep the free tier useful enough to establish a real operational habit. Charge for depth, capacity and service—not
access to basic security or the ability to retrieve customer data. Add-ons must have a visible total price and their
dependencies included before checkout.


Subscription revenue and setup revenue are reported separately. Annual prepayment may improve cash flow but
creates a delivery obligation; it is not immediately earned profit. Do not provide unlimited messaging, AI, storage or
visits in a low-price bundle.


No escrow, proprietary tokens or payments held for customers are assumed. Customers can record logistics
collections in their own finance workflow; moving third-party funds through LOAD|BY|TON would require a separate
approved design.

07 — Unit economics and sustainability
Measure contribution per tenant: net subscription revenue minus payment fees, allocated compute/storage,
messaging/AI subsidies, support labor, connector variable cost and refunds. Track onboarding contribution separately
after technician labor, travel and migration work. Founder labor is still a cost even when unpaid.


Illustrative Starter scenario: AED 149 revenue minus AED 5 payment cost, AED 15 infrastructure allocation, AED 10
automated-processing allowance and AED 30 support allocation leaves AED 89 monthly contribution, approximately
60%, before fixed engineering, administration, tax and acquisition. These are invented scenario inputs, not supplier
quotes. A rise to AED 80 support cost leaves only AED 39 contribution. This sensitivity makes standardized onboarding
essential.


At AED 89 contribution, a hypothetical AED 350 acquisition cost takes about 3.9 months to recover, excluding
onboarding losses. At AED 39 it takes about 9 months. Use actual retained-cohort contribution before approving
discounts. Simple LTV approximations are unreliable with small samples or changing churn.


For the portfolio, break-even paid tenants = monthly fixed costs divided by weighted average contribution per paid
tenant, after including the free-tier subsidy. Model free accounts explicitly: 1,000 free tenants at a hypothetical AED
3/month cost AED 3,000/month. “Start with zero dollars” can mean founder-led validation and manual concierge work;
a reliable commercial platform still has operating costs.

09 — Product and organization structure
The shared platform core comprises identity, organizations, permissions, master records, documents, tasks,
communications, audit, integration management and reporting. Operational domains are Freight, Transport,
Warehouse, Delivery, Customs and specialist extensions. Commercial domains are CRM, rates/contracts and
quotations. Financial domains are job costing, invoicing, collections, payables and either accounting integration or a
subsequently validated native ledger.


Treat a tenant, legal entity and branch as different objects. A tenant is the subscription/customer boundary. Legal
entities have independent financial and tax identities. Branches belong to entities. A customer’s warehouse, carrier and
supplier accounts are parties, not additional platform tenants unless those businesses independently subscribe.


Staff roles: owner, platform commercial administrator, sales, pricing, coordinator, dispatcher, warehouse
supervisor/operator, finance clerk/controller, HR administrator, auditor and portal user. One person may hold multiple
roles. Purchasing an entitlement does not grant every staff member access to it.

12 — Core workflow: enquiry to collection
    1. Capture enquiry from form, email or approved messaging channel; preserve source and deduplicate.

    2. Validate route, cargo, timing, parties and missing information; assign a responsible salesperson.

    3. Obtain rates; track supplier validity, currency, charge basis and exclusions.

    4. Prepare versioned quote; approval required below margin threshold or above credit exposure limit.

    5. Record acceptance and agreed scope; convert to one job without duplicate customer entry.

    6. Plan route legs and suppliers; issue authorized bookings and purchase orders.

    7. Record milestones and evidence; create exceptions with owner, deadline and escalation.

    8. Collect delivery/release proof; check billable events and outstanding supplier costs.

    9. Generate invoice from approved charges; reconcile external accounting identifiers if connected.

   10. Track collection and match receipts; close financially after costs and adjustments are reconciled.


Operational completion and financial closure are separate statuses. A delivered job may still have unreceived supplier
invoices, an open claim or overdue receivables. Owners must see those distinctions.




13 — Freight and commercial specification
CRM: organizations/contacts, source, relationship owner, activities, opportunities, tenders, lost reasons, consent
preferences and credit review. Rates: origin/destination, mode, service, supplier, validity, currency, units, minimums,
surcharges, tier breaks and equipment/cargo restrictions. Quotations: revisions, charge lines, exclusions, validity,
approval, acceptance and conversion lineage.


Freight jobs: parties, incoterm, mode, cargo pieces/dimensions/weight, equipment, routing, planned/actual milestones,
booking references, master/house links, carrier/agent, attachments, release conditions and charge allocations. Keep
weight and volume units explicit. Chargeable-weight calculations use validated mode/provider rules rather than a
single universal formula.


Documents: commercial invoice, packing list, booking confirmation, transport documents, delivery order and shipment
instructions as applicable. Version templates and distinguish a generated draft from an externally issued authoritative
document. Record who approved or issued it; avoid implying the platform itself has authority to issue every regulated
document.


Consolidation allocations require an explicit basis such as volume, weight or agreed allocation; totals must reconcile to
the master cost. Margin should show quotation estimate, updated expected cost and actual reconciled outcome.
14 — Transport, delivery and COD specification
Transport: customer orders, trip legs, load/capacity assignment, equipment, vehicle/driver availability, compliance
expiries, maintenance blocks, supplier transport, route estimates, actual events, fuel/tolls, POD, detention and
profitability. Driver workflows must be usable with large touch targets and poor connectivity.


Delivery: parcel/barcode IDs, merchant order references, addresses, geocoding confidence, assignment, attempt
events, customer instructions, reason codes, returns and proof. Delivery proof captures authorized
signature/photo/time; optional location requires policy and permission. A notification failure does not reverse a
successful delivery.


COD: collection expected, collected, handed over, reconciled, merchant payable and settled are separate events.
Require evidence and dual approval for cash adjustments. Example: three AED 100 collections and AED 250 handover
create a visible AED 50 shortage, not a silently reduced merchant settlement. LOAD|BY|TON records this workflow;
handling funds itself is outside this blueprint.


Route optimization and live GPS need providers, validated constraints and actual telemetry. A map animation is not live
tracking. Offline events retain local IDs and device time; the server resolves duplicates and conflicts without overwriting
financial or approval records.




15 — Warehouse and fulfilment specification
WMS: sites/zones/bins, owner-specific SKUs, units and conversions, receipts/ASN, inspection, quarantine, putaway,
reservation, picking, packing, dispatch, transfers, counts, adjustments, lot/serial/expiry and returns. FEFO/FIFO are
configured policies; allocation changes need traceability.


3PL custody stock is separated by owner even if the same SKU exists in the same building. Custody quantities are not
automatically the warehouse company’s balance-sheet inventory. Movement records are immutable; corrections are
compensating movements. Prevent negative available stock unless an explicitly authorized workflow supports it.


Activity billing uses contract versions: pallet/day storage, receipt, pick, packing, labor or special handling. Define daily
storage snapshot, unit conversion, minimum fees and partial-day treatment. A billing preview links every line to events
and flags missing tariff matches; duplicate runs cannot duplicate charges.


Fulfilment: merchant order import, external ID deduplication, reservation, pick/pack, label, shipment, tracking,
cancellation and return inspection. If a channel cancels an already dispatched order, create a return/intercept
exception; do not simply restore stock. Validate webhook ordering and reconcile channel totals.
16 — Customs and UAE readiness
Customs workspace: case reference, shipment link, importer/exporter identifiers, commodity lines, supporting
evidence, permit checklist, declaration draft, inspection, deposit/guarantee tracking, duty/service charges and
approved release evidence. Staff record authority responses and their timestamps.


Dubai Trade supplies a Mirsal 2 environment for Dubai Customs processes; registration and enabled services are
prerequisites. This blueprint does not establish that a public integration API is available to LOAD|BY|TON. Begin with
controlled document/export handover; implement direct connectivity only through confirmed official access and
customer authority. Dubai Trade and registration guidance.


UAE eInvoicing is structured invoice exchange, not simply a PDF. Build an adapter to a verified accredited service
provider, using the current mandatory-field and format requirements. Capture submission, acknowledgment, rejection,
correction and reconciliation states. Provider selection and applicable implementation obligations need current
customer-specific review. Do not call LOAD|BY|TON an accredited provider without accreditation. MoF programme and
ASP register.


Country configuration includes AED and multicurrency, English/Arabic documents and RTL layouts, tax identifiers, legal
entity fields, tax-code mappings, audit records and configurable work calendars. Logistics tax treatment depends on
the transaction and supporting facts; do not apply one generic VAT rule to all freight lines. Payroll/WPS, customs,
dangerous-goods and other statutory capabilities remain separately reviewed projects.




17 — Finance and accounting integrity
Initial paid release should support robust job costing, operational invoices and collections plus one validated
accounting integration. Native full accounting is a later release unless it can meet a finance-controller acceptance
pack. This keeps a clear difference between “finance visibility” and a complete statutory accounting system.


Native ledger requirements: balanced double-entry journals; chart of accounts; AP/AR; bank/cash reconciliation;
currency and FX handling; credit notes; allocations; period locks; authorized reversals; tax reports; trial balance;
balance sheet; profit/loss; opening balances; intercompany controls if sold. Posted entries are immutable and
corrected by approved reversal/adjustment.


Expected job margin = agreed revenue minus known and estimated attributable costs. Actual margin = reconciled
recognized revenue minus posted attributable costs. Display remaining accruals and FX effects. Customer deposits,
pass-through charges and revenue recognition require accountant-approved policies; they are not all sales income.


With external accounting, define system of record for customers, invoices, tax, payment and ledger. Use an integration
outbox and external IDs. A retry must never post a second invoice. Owner reports must flag unsynchronized invoices
and never label partial operational data as reconciled books.
18 — Work, communication and AI
Every queue item needs an owner, priority, due time, business-calendar SLA, linked records and evidence.
Department handovers require acceptance or a visible unassigned queue. Escalation should identify who can resolve
the problem, not send repeated alerts to everyone.


Shared inbox: conversation identity, company/record links, assignment, collision warnings, consent preferences,
template versions, delivery status and failed-message handling. WhatsApp automation requires approved provider
access and current policy compliance; it is not guaranteed by connecting a personal number. Call intake initially uses
staff capture; recording/transcription needs explicit policy, permissions and an appropriate telephony provider.


AI can extract draft fields from documents, suggest enquiry classification, draft replies, summarize job exceptions and
explain reports with linked source evidence. Confidence and source provenance are visible. The AI reads only records
the acting user can access. Apply prompt-injection defenses to uploaded documents; extracted text cannot authorize
system actions.


Human approval is required for prices sent to customers, supplier commitments, payments, posted journals, customs
submissions, payroll, inventory adjustments and data deletion. Automation can execute low-risk reminders within
approved templates and limits. Meter AI cost by tenant, use quotas, redact unnecessary personal data and maintain a
deterministic fallback.




19 — Three architectural planes
Commerce plane: website, sector diagnosis, catalog, checkout and account acquisition. Control plane: tenant
lifecycle, subscriptions, entitlements, payment reconciliation, provisioning, domains, staff support grants and rollout.
Operating plane: the customer’s business records and workflows.


The control plane grants commercial entitlements; the operating plane enforces them with membership, permissions
and business rules. Separate service credentials and privileged administrative access. Operational tenant staff cannot
edit platform subscription facts through an ordinary company API.


Start with a modular monolith for transactional business logic, plus separate background workers. This is an
architecture recommendation for affordability and manageable operations, not a claim that every scale requires it.
Keep clean domain interfaces so high-volume tracking or integration workers can be separated when actual load
justifies it.


Proposed stack: TypeScript web/PWA interface and API, PostgreSQL transactional database, private object storage,
durable queue workers, identity service, email/messaging/payment adapters and centralized monitoring.
Hosting/managed-service choices require a current cost, security, region and recovery assessment before
procurement. Do not depend on free hosting allowances as a production capacity guarantee.

21 — Tenant isolation and security
Use database-enforced row policies or equivalently strong tenant enforcement backed by restricted runtime roles.
Tenant identity comes from authenticated membership, not a browser-supplied tenant_id. Privileged workers must
explicitly establish tenant scope. Include isolation tests for database queries, exports, files, search, caches, queues
and AI retrieval.
Private file storage uses tenant-scoped paths, access checks and short-lived signed access. Scan uploads, restrict
types/size and prevent executable content from rendering as trusted application content. Encrypt transport and stored
data through the selected infrastructure. Manage secrets outside code and logs.


Require MFA for platform administrators and sensitive company roles. Support customer-controlled role grants,
session revocation, audit trails and separation of payment preparation from approval. Field staff receive time-limited
access with the customer’s permission and an audit record of operator identity.


Establish privacy notices, customer/provider responsibilities, retention schedules, subprocessor disclosures, breach
procedures and data access/export/deletion workflows. UAE data protection requirements and cross-border
processing need deployment-specific review. Federal and special-jurisdiction requirements may differ; this is a design
obligation, not legal certification. UAE official data protection overview.




22 — Payments, entitlements and lifecycle
Separate subscription state from payment attempt state. Maintain catalog versions, billing customer IDs, order lines,
setup charges, subscription periods, discounts and effective entitlement dates. Never grant access solely from a
browser success redirect.


Payment processing sequence: create immutable order; request provider session; validate signature and
amount/currency/reference on server event; persist event; apply idempotent subscription transition; queue
provisioning; reconcile against provider records. Handle duplicate events, out-of-order delivery, delayed success,
refunds, chargebacks and changed payment methods.


Entitlement evaluation checks identity, membership, role/record scope, product right, quota and business-state
preconditions. Quota increments are transactional to stop concurrent requests exceeding limits. Meter billable usage
through immutable usage events and reconcile counters.


Downgrade at renewal after previewing limits and workflow consequences. Paid-period cancellation retains agreed
access. A published payment grace period precedes restriction. Exceeding storage or record limits blocks new excess
usage, not reading existing records. Open cargo and posted finance records need a controlled transition path. Define
export access and retention duration in the contract; do not invent unlimited perpetual hosting.




23 — Provisioning and 24-hour activation
State progression: payment pending → verified → information pending if required → queued → provisioning →
validation → ready → onboarding. Failed and cancelled are explicit branches with reasons, owner and recovery
actions. The free tenant is reused.
Each provisioning step has a unique idempotency key: reserve slug; apply entitlement version; load configuration;
attach routing; verify TLS; validate owner access; run health/isolation checks; send readiness notice; create
onboarding work. Retrying resumes completed steps safely. Send notifications through an outbox after durable state
changes.


Advertise standard branded activation within 24 hours only after the platform can actually support it. The clock begins
after verified payment and minimum setup information. Track p50/p95 duration, aged queue, failure rate and manual
intervention. Custom domains, migration, field visits and external-provider approvals have separate milestones and
conditions.


Keep the standard subdomain working during custom-domain setup. Domain ownership, host routing and certificates
must be verified; remove mappings safely on disconnection and prevent takeover. Do not use hostnames alone for
access control. Set the compensation policy for provider-caused activation delay before making the promise public.




24 — Reliability, APIs and recovery
Use transactional outbox events for business changes that must trigger external work. Consumers deduplicate event
IDs, retry with limits and route permanent failures to a staff-visible queue. Webhooks must be authenticated and
replay-resistant. APIs support scoped credentials, pagination, versioning, limits, idempotent mutation and trace IDs.


Connector states: requested, available, connected, validating, live, degraded and disconnected. Marketing says “live”
only after a tested tenant flow is available. Connector acceptance includes retry, duplicate, expired authorization,
provider outage, partial failure and reconciliation.


Proposed general availability targets: 99.9% monthly application availability; p95 ordinary interactive reads under 1.5
seconds at published pilot load; shared-platform RPO at most 1 hour and RTO at most 8 hours. These are engineering
objectives to validate before contractual adoption, not present guarantees. More demanding recovery is separately
priced.


Restore tests must verify a tenant’s database records, object files, audit history and accounting/stock invariants. A
backup job showing green is insufficient. Track queue age, provider errors, certificate expiry, latency, worker backlog,
database saturation and tenant cost. Roll out schema/application changes through staging, canaries and reversible
flags, preserving old event compatibility.




25 — Onboarding and migration operations
Basic setup is a bounded service: entity/branding, purchased templates, owner/staff invitation, initial customer import,
one remote training session and acceptance checklist. Publish duration and record allowance. On-site visits, travel,
printer/barcode setup and historical migration are scoped additions.
Migration sequence: source inventory; permission and export; field mapping; quality review; dry-run import; customer
validation; opening-balance/stock reconciliation; change freeze; final delta; cutover; monitored first transactions. Keep
source files and import reports securely for the agreed period. Do not import unverified opening balances into posted
finance.


Preserve current workflows through staged adoption. Begin new jobs in LOAD|BY|TON while older jobs close in the
existing system, or use a controlled cutoff. Avoid indefinite dual entry. Agree the system of record per function and
publish the fallback if cutover fails.


Technician work orders contain scope, prerequisites, appointment, access expiry, evidence, acceptance and
unresolved issues. A visit completed is not the same as customer activation; activation means staff can complete the
purchased end-to-end workflow.




26 — Internal team and service governance
Five staff could initially cover: product/operations owner; engineering lead; QA/security/release owner;
onboarding/customer success; sales/support coordination. This is a responsibility map, not proof that five people can
build and support the whole portfolio. Deep accounting and sector compliance need specialist review. Provide backup
coverage for every critical role.


Weekly operating review: paid activation, unresolved critical defects, customer exceptions, overdue provisioning,
support hours per tenant, connector incidents, contribution and churn causes. Assign an incident commander and
customer communications owner during outages.


Proposed support tiers: urgent data/security or core-workflow failure; major partial disruption; normal assistance;
service request. Publish response commitments and supported hours after staffing capacity is established. Response
time does not equal resolution time. Low-cost plans cannot implicitly carry unlimited 24/7 human support.


Partner onboarding/referral services may become a distribution channel. Partner compensation should follow verified
collected revenue and account for refunds and cancellations. Disclose commercial relationships and prohibit
exaggerated compliance or product promises.




27 — Owner dashboard and management reports
Owner view: estimated vs actual margin; unbilled completed jobs; overdue receivables; cash collection due; supplier
exposure; exceptions approaching deadline; delayed milestones; document holds; team capacity; customer
concentration; subscription usage. Every card links to actionable records and a responsible person.
Sales reports: enquiry conversion, quote win/loss and rate validity. Freight: jobs by stage, margin variance, accrual
completeness, detention exposure. Transport: utilization, on-time service, cost/trip and vehicle downtime. Warehouse:
stock accuracy, occupied space, expiry risk and unbilled activity. Delivery: first-attempt completion, return reasons and
COD differences. Finance: aging, collection cycle, reconciled balances and FX variance.


Platform SaaS reports: activated tenants, free-to-paid conversion by cohort, MRR excluding setup, logo/revenue churn,
payment recovery, onboarding hours, support cost, infrastructure cost and contribution. Define an activated forwarding
customer as a genuine quote converted to a job with recorded cost and invoice workflow, not merely a login.




28 — Go-to-market and growth
Start with founder-led and partner-assisted selling to a single sector. Offer a short discovery using the customer’s
actual workflow, then a free workspace and scoped paid pilot. Show one concrete result: jobs delivered but not yet
invoiced, or costs missed before invoicing. Avoid claiming cost savings before measuring a baseline.


Acquire through UAE business relationships, sector associations, practical workshops, referrals and accurate sector
search pages. Public content should answer operational problems with real examples. Case studies require customer
consent and verified results. Automated outreach must respect permissions and provider rules.


The free product can create growth through customer invitations, useful quotation outputs and portal visibility, but
default customer documents should not become advertising billboards. Owner-approved attribution and referral
prompts are more credible. Do not spend on broad campaigns until retained usage and contribution support acquisition
costs.


Before a paid sale, send: scope, plan schedule, setup statement, integration status, support hours, data agreement,
cancellation/export policy and acceptance criteria. Sales promises must match the released capability catalog.

31 — Main risks and controls
Scope overload: release one complete sector before adding more. Low-price support losses: limit setup and
standardize imports. Free-tier abuse: verification, quotas and fair scheduling. Incorrect accounting: specialist-reviewed
posting and reconciliation. Integration dependency: explicit status, outage handling and manual handover. Security
failure: tenant isolation and privileged-access controls. Regulatory overstatement: publish precise capabilities and
separate approval dependencies. Customer lock-in fears: usable exports and clear exit policy.


Avoid separate forks per customer. Branding, fields, roles and workflows should be configuration. Paid customization
must use versioned extensions or an explicitly supported dedicated offering; otherwise hundreds of accounts become
hundreds of maintenance projects.




32 — Decisions required before implementation
Approve the first sector and end-to-end scope; the contracting entity and payment merchant; hosting/region and
recovery budget; pilot plan limits; accounting system of record; setup and visit coverage; support hours; free-tier
retention/exit policy; initial connector provider; and specialist review owners. Verify domain ownership and final vector
assets before deployment.


The recommended first product is LOAD|BY|TON Forwarding Starter: enquiry → quote → job → supplier costs →
document/milestone control → invoice → collection visibility, with a permanent free entry tier and branded activation.
Build it so a small logistics owner can trust one complete daily workflow. Expand only when that trust, delivery quality
and economics are established.
